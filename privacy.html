Privacy Policy

Effective 11 November 2026

The short version

  • Chora has no accounts, no servers of its own, no analytics and no tracking. I never receive your data.
  • Almost everything Chora touches stays on your Mac: your calendar, camera, audio, clipboard and shelf.
  • Chora only goes online for the things listed under What goes online: lyrics, album art, update checks, and Google Tasks if you sign in.
  • Nothing is sold, shared for advertising, or used to build a profile of you.

Who this covers

This policy covers the Chora app for macOS (currently distributed as boringNotch) and this website. Chora is built and maintained by Ronald Wen, an independent developer. It is based on the open-source boring.notch project, and its full source code is public, so anything in this policy can be checked against the code.

Permissions Chora asks for

macOS asks you before Chora can use any of these, and you can turn each one off at any time in System Settings → Privacy & Security. Features that need a permission you've declined simply stop working; the rest of the app is unaffected.

Calendars and Reminders

Used to show your events and reminders in the notch and on the Calendar tab. When you drag an event to move or resize it, or choose Delete, Chora makes that change through macOS's calendar system. If that calendar is synced to iCloud, Google or another account, macOS syncs the change there, the same as if you'd made it in the Calendar app. Chora does not copy your events anywhere else and never sends them over the internet itself.

Camera

Used only for the mirror, a live preview of your camera in the notch. The camera turns on when you open the mirror and off when you close it. Nothing is recorded, saved, photographed or sent anywhere.

System audio recording

macOS calls this "record your system audio." Chora uses it for two things: the music visualizer, which reacts to what's playing, and the volume mixer, which sets each app's volume separately. The audio is processed in memory as it plays, in real time, and then discarded. Chora never records audio to a file, never stores it, and never sends it anywhere. It does not use your microphone.

Automation (controlling Music and Spotify)

Chora asks macOS for permission to talk to the Music and Spotify apps, so it can show what's playing and let you play, pause and skip from the notch. It reads the song title, artist, album, artwork and playback position, and only from those apps.

Data stored on your Mac

These stay on your Mac. They are never uploaded by Chora.

  • Settings. Your preferences, such as your last tab, timer lengths and appearance, are stored in Chora's preferences file.
  • Clipboard history. Chora saves your last 50 copied items, including copied images, in its Application Support folder so they survive a restart. Chora doesn't currently detect passwords copied from password managers. If you copy one, delete it from the clipboard history, or clear the whole history.
  • Shelf. Files you drop on the notch are kept as references (macOS bookmarks) to the original files, not copies. Chora only shares a shelf file when you choose a destination from the macOS share menu, such as AirDrop or Mail. The destination you pick handles it from there.
  • Album art cache. Artwork is cached on disk so it doesn't have to be downloaded again.
  • Google sign-in token. If you connect Google Tasks, the sign-in token Google issues is stored in Chora's preferences on this Mac. See Google Tasks.

What goes online

Chora connects to these services directly from your Mac, and only for these reasons. Like any internet connection, each one sees your IP address. Each service's own privacy policy applies to what it receives.

Lyrics: LRCLIB

When lyrics are on and a song plays, Chora sends the song's title and artist to lrclib.net, a free public lyrics database, to look up its synced lyrics. Nothing else about you or your library is sent.

Album art

Chora downloads album artwork from the address your music app provides. That's usually your music service's own image servers, for example Spotify's.

Animations: LottieFiles

If you turn off the live music visualizer, Chora shows a small animation in its place, downloaded from LottieFiles. If you add a custom animation from a web address, it's downloaded from that address.

Updates: GitHub

About once an hour, Chora checks for a new version by downloading a small update feed from GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com). When you install an update, it downloads the app from GitHub Releases. These requests identify the app and its version, as every app update request does. Chora does not send a system profile (hardware or usage details) with update checks. Every update is cryptographically signed, and Chora refuses any update without a valid signature. You can turn off automatic checks in Settings.

YouTube Music

If you use the YouTube Music desktop app (Pear Desktop), Chora talks to it over a local connection on your own Mac (localhost). That connection never leaves your computer.

Google Tasks

Connecting Google Tasks is optional. Here's exactly how it works:

  • Signing in. Chora opens Google's sign-in page in your web browser. You sign in with Google, not with Chora, and Chora never sees your Google password. Google then returns you to Chora through a temporary local address on your own Mac (127.0.0.1). That address only exists for the moment you're signing in. The sign-in uses PKCE, a standard safeguard that stops other apps from intercepting it.
  • What Chora can access. Chora asks for one permission, the Google Tasks scope (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/tasks), and uses it only to show your task lists and tasks on the Calendar tab. It doesn't request access to your email, contacts, Drive or any other Google data.
  • Where the data goes. Your tasks travel directly between Google's servers and your Mac. They never pass through any server run by me or anyone else, and they aren't saved to disk. Chora fetches them fresh while the Calendar tab is open.
  • Storage. The sign-in token Google issues is kept in Chora's preferences on your Mac. That lets you stay signed in without signing in every time.
  • Disconnecting. Choose Sign out in Chora's settings to delete the token from your Mac. You can also revoke Chora's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Chora's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google user data is used only to show your tasks to you in the app. It is not transferred to anyone, not used for advertising, not used to train any machine learning or AI model, and not read by any person.

What Chora does not do

  • No analytics, crash reporting, telemetry or usage tracking of any kind.
  • No advertising, and no ad or tracking libraries.
  • No accounts with Chora, and no Chora servers that store anything about you.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of your data.
  • No recording of your camera, screen or audio.

This website

This site is a set of static pages hosted on GitHub Pages. It has no cookies, no analytics, no ads and no trackers. GitHub, as the host, may log technical details such as your IP address when you visit; see GitHub's privacy statement. The changelog page loads the release notes from GitHub as you view it.

Children

Chora isn't directed at children under 13, and it doesn't knowingly collect personal information from anyone. Because Chora has no servers or accounts, there's no personal information held about any user, of any age.

Your choices

  • Turn off any permission in System Settings → Privacy & Security.
  • Turn off lyrics or automatic update checks in Chora's settings.
  • Delete single items from the clipboard history, or clear it all.
  • Disconnect Google Tasks by choosing Sign out in Chora's settings.
  • Remove everything by quitting Chora and deleting the app. Its saved data lives in ~/Library/Application Support and ~/Library/Preferences under the app's name, and you can delete it there too.

Changes to this policy

If Chora starts handling data in a new way, this page will be updated before or with the release that changes it, and the change will be noted in the changelog. The effective date at the top always shows the latest version.
